This slows down the growth of heat-loving tropical plants.How to Grow Papayas in Fern ForestGrowing papayas here is a distinct challenge because they absolutely hate wet feet, cold air, and low nutrients. Left to the natural elements, they will succumb to root rot or produce small, deformed ("cat-faced") fruit. If you have a true pocket of deep dirt or brought-in soil in Fern Forest, you can skip building mounds out of pocket rock.However, you still have to deal with the area’s extreme rain (often 150+ inches a year) and cooler upland air. Because papaya roots drown easily in stagnant water, having dirt changes how you plant to keep those roots breathing.How to Use Your Dirt Safely for PapayasDo Not Plant in a "Puka" (Hole): In Fern Forest, digging a hole in the dirt down to the underlying solid pahoehoe lava creates a literal underground swimming pool. The rain will fill that hole, sit on the rock, and rot your papaya roots within days.Amend for Ultimate Drainage: Even though you have dirt, it needs to be incredibly loose. Mix your dirt 50/50 with black volcanic cinder or coarse sand. This ensures that water rushes straight past the roots instead of packing the dirt into tight, oxygen-deprived mud.Plant on a Slight Rise: Even with dirt, plant your papaya seeds or starts on a slight hill or gentle slope so surface water naturally sheets away from the main trunk.High-Elevation Varieties to TryAt 2,000 feet, the chilly night air can cause standard papayas to develop low sugar (bland taste) or "cat-faced" (deformed) fruit. Since you are growing from seed, try to source these specific varieties which handle the cold and rain better:Kapoho Solo: This is the classic Big Island standard. It is explicitly adapted to rainy, windward conditions and maintains its sweet, yellow flesh better than other varieties in wet weather.Waimanalo Low-Bearing (X-77): A yellow-fleshed variety that bears fruit very close to the ground. Because it stays shorter longer, it is easier to shield from chilly upland winds.Red Lady: A highly resilient, larger Mexican-hybrid type. While not a traditional Hawaiian solo papaya, it is famous for tolerating cooler, suboptimal temperatures while remaining incredibly sweet.Pro-Tip for Fern Forest SeedsIf you want the absolute best chance of success, buy a highly delicious, locally grown papaya from a farm stand in Mountain View or Volcano and save those seeds.
