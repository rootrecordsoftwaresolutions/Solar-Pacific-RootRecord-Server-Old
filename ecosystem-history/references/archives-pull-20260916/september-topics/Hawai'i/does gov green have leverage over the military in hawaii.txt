Governor Josh Green does not have direct command leverage over the U.S. military, but he wields significant political leverage through state-owned land. Because the Army relies on tens of thousands of acres of state trust lands for crucial training, Green is able to use the lease renewal process to negotiate for billions in federal investment.Key factors in this dynamic include:Land Leases: The U.S. Army's 65-year leases on approximately 29,000 acres of state land at the Pōhakuloa Training Area on Hawaiʻi Island and several sites on Oʻahu are set to expire in 2029.State Leverage: State authorities, like the Board of Land and Natural Resources, previously rejected the Army's environmental impact statements for these lands. This forces the military to the negotiating table to secure future access.Federal Counter-Leverage: The U.S. federal government possesses the power of eminent domain. The Army has explored the possibility of unilateral condemnation of these properties in the interest of national security, a threat that balances the negotiating scales and pushes the state toward compromise.Demands & Agreements: Green’s administration has laid out a list of demands in exchange for the leases. These include billions in federal investments for housing, environmental cleanup, and local infrastructure, as well as the return of certain lands for community use.While Governor Green controls the Hawaiʻi National Guard for domestic state emergencies, overall operational control of active-duty U.S. Armed Forces resides with the President and the Department of Defense. You can track the ongoing discussions by checking updates from the Office of the Governor and local outlets like Civil Beat.
