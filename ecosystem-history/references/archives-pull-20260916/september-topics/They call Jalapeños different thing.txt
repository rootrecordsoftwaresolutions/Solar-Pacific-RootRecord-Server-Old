Here is what a red jalapeño is called across its different stages:Fresh Red JalapeñosHuachinango: In many regions of Mexico, a fresh, fully ripened red jalapeño is traditionally called a huachinango (named after the red snapper fish due to its bright color).Cuaresmeño / Chile Gordo: While these terms are often used for green jalapeños, some regions still use them to describe the thick, fleshy red version.Smoked and Dried Red JalapeñosOnce a jalapeño turns red on the vine, it is harvested, dried, and smoked. This is where the name shifts entirely:Chipotle: This is the universal name for a smoked, dried red jalapeño. The word comes from the Nahuatl (Aztec) word chīpotlei, meaning "smoked chili".Chipotle Morita: This is a specific type of chipotle. It is a red jalapeño that has been smoked for a shorter period, leaving it soft, pliable, and a deep purple-red color with a fruitier flavor.Chipotle Meco: This version is smoked for much longer until it becomes completely dry, stiff, and a dusty tan or gray-brown color. It has an intensely smoky, earthy flavor.Dried (But Unsmoked) Red JalapeñosSeco / Chile Seco: If a red jalapeño is simply dehydrated in the sun or an oven without being smoked, it is referred to generally as a chile seco (which translates to "dry chile").Would you like to know more about how to use chipotle moritas versus mecos in cooking, or are you interested in a specific recipe that calls for them?AI responses may include mistakes.
