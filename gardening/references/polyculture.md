# Polyculture (default)

This desk designs **more than one species sharing space and time**. Monoculture
is the exception, and only when they asked for it.

## What to name

| Pattern | USDA / official hook | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Multi-story / mixed agroforest | NRCS CPS **379** (and **612** on a bare site). Pacific Islands Mixed Agroforest spec. | Hawaiʻi / Pacific woody + understory stacks |
| Alley cropping | CPS **311** | Rows of trees with annuals or perennials between |
| Silvopasture | CPS **381** | Trees + forage + livestock on purpose |
| Windbreak / hedgerow | CPS **380** / **422** | Edges that also feed insects and break wind |
| Cover crop / cocktail | CPS **340**; CTAHR tropical cover-crop database | Soil armor, N, weed stall — mix species |
| Forest farming / NTFP | NAC forest farming; PIA agroforest NTFP notes | Shade crops under a canopy |
| Traditional Hawaiian | CTAHR kalo; UH/CTAHR writing on loʻi and dryland field systems | Kalo, ʻuala, maiʻa, ulu — not a corn belt template |
| Three sisters | Documented Indigenous intercrop (corn/bean/squash) in temperate extension lit | Zones where maize is a summer annual — **not** a Hawaiʻi default |

Pacific Islands mixed agroforest specs set **diversity and native-share rules**
(genera counts, plants per acre, native fraction, NTFP caps). Read the current
eFOTG PDF. Do not recite numbers from memory.

## How to answer

1. Site: which island. If **Hawaiʻi Island**, climates first
   (`references/big-island.md`): windward/leeward + elevation. Then ZIP → 2023
   PHZM. Soil only if WSS/CTAHR has it. Do not collapse the island to one zone.
2. Canopy first: one or more overstory species that belong there.
3. Fill strata: midstory, shrubs, ground, nitrogen fixer or mulch coppice,
   living cover. Name functions, not a seed-company “trio.”
4. Stagger harvest and residue. Do not clear the floor for a single cash row
   unless they insisted.
5. Refuse invasives. Check HDOA / CTAHR before a Hawaiʻi plant list.

## What not to do

- Do not treat USDA zone as a planting calendar. On Hawaiʻi Island, wet
  windward and dry leeward can share a hardiness band. Look up the Rainfall
  Atlas pixel.
- Do not copy Midwest cover-crop cocktails onto a Kona slope, or a wet-windward
  mix onto a dry leeward coast.
- Do not invent “companion planting” chemistry. If CTAHR or NRCS did not
  publish the pairing, say it is unlisted.
- Do not design sun-grown monoculture coffee, pineapple blocks, or single-cultivar
  macadamia as the skill default.
