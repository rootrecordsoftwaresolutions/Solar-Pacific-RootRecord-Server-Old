# Hawaiʻi Island (Big Island) climates

True statement: this island is **not one climate**. Planting has to follow
**this slope**, not “Hawaiʻi” or a USDA zone number.

## What is on the record

UH Mānoa Rainfall Atlas (Giambelluca et al.): the Hawaiian Islands have one of
the most diverse rainfall patterns on earth. Mountains, persistent trades, land
heating and cooling, and a stable layer near 7,000 ft make mean rainfall change
fast over short distances. Interactive map:
http://rainfall.geography.hawaii.edu/

Same team, BAMS 2013: statewide atlas annual means (1978–2007) run from
**204 mm** near Mauna Kea summit to **10,271 mm** near Big Bog, Maui. The dry
end of that published range is on this island. The wet extreme they cite is
Maui. Do not invent a Hilo inch count.

USDA 2023 Plant Hardiness Zone Map: average annual **extreme minimum**
temperature. Not rain. Not a climate class. Zones 12 and 13 are on the Hawaiʻi
and Puerto Rico maps. Two coastal ZIPs can share a hardiness band and still
differ in rain by a lot. Look the ZIP up. Do not guess.

Juvik, Singleton, and Clarke 1978 (*Climate and water balance on the island of
Hawaiʻi*, NOAA Mauna Loa Observatory 20th anniversary) is a real chapter. Price
et al. 2008 cite it for MAP minus PET. **This desk does not have that PDF.**
It does not assert how many Köppen boxes they ticked.

## What is not on the record here

“All but 2 / 3 / 5 of Earth’s climates” is slogan math. Earth does not have one
official integer of climates. Do not say it.

Magazine and tourism pages repeat “10 of 14” Köppen subgroups from that 1978
chapter. Blogs give other leftovers (including 8 of 13). Until we read the
chapter or classify the atlas ourselves, **no leftover number goes in an
answer.**

Do not pin Köppen codes on towns (Hilo = Af, Kawaihae = BWh, Waimea = Cfb,
Kona = Am) as if this desk mapped them.
